Upright roll rig: two steppers, two lasers, 3 mm to the crossover

The build plan for Concept D right-side up, like the pod: the yoke sits under a fixed steel plate on an aluminum beam. A lead-screw stepper at each end sets gap and roll, and two Baumer lasers, one per pole, close the loop. Pitch is locked by the joints. The sweep covers 3 mm up to the crossover gap, where the magnet's pull equals the weight of the moving parts, about 9.7 mm. A counterweight keeps that range well preloaded. This replaces the Inverted Roll Rig as the plan of record.

Axes

  • Roll is rotation about the into-the-page axis: left pole closer than right. controlled
  • Pitch is rotation about the horizontal axis: front edge versus back edge. locked by joints On a 1" deep yoke, 1° of pitch changes force by about 0.2% at 5 mm.
  • Yaw is rotation about the vertical axis. Gap doesn't depend on it. ignored
  • Every drawing is a side view, with the yoke's long direction running left to right.

Scope: 3 mm to the crossover

The magnet pulls the yoke up (F) and gravity pulls the moving parts down (W ≈ 6 kg, 59 N). The sweep stays where F ≥ W. There, the net load always points up, so the cells, joints, and screw nuts are loaded in one direction only. Gaps beyond the crossover are left for later.

Net upward load on the screws vs gap (total for both sides)

F(g) is from scripts/yoke_model.py (Ansys, 0 A, 5–15 mm). W = 59 N, and C is the counterweight's upward pull. Without a counterweight, the net load drops below about 15 N past 8 mm and reaches zero at 9.7 mm, so the top of the sweep would sit on nut and joint play. With 3 kg of counterweight, the crossover moves to about 16 mm and there's still about 25 N of preload at 9.7 mm. That keeps the whole sweep firmly loaded.

The crossover is a property of the rig, not the pod. 9.7 mm is where the magnet carries this rig's 6 kg of moving parts. The pod's equilibrium gap depends on each yoke's share of the pod's weight. F(z) is the same physics either way, so the measured curve applies to both. Just don't read the rig's crossover as the pod's operating gap.

Load path, bottom to top (each side)

  1. Base plateAluminum. The steppers bolt to it.
  2. StepperA 48 mm NEMA 17, at least 150 mm below the poles. Its internal bearing takes the axial load.
  3. Lead screw + nutTr8×2, 10 µm per full step, self-locking. The net upward load preloads the nut in one direction across the whole sweep.
  4. MGN12 carriageKeeps the nut from turning and moving sideways. The rail takes side load and pitch moment, so the cells don't.
  5. Aluminum pushrodNon-magnetic link up to the cell. It's in tension, holding the beam down against the magnet.
  6. Load cell (YZC-133)Fixed end to the pushrod bracket, free end to the joint. Mount it with the label arrow pointing up, along the magnet's pull. It stays in tension and never crosses zero inside the sweep.
  7. JointLeft: pin. Right: pin in a slot. Both pin axes run into the page, so roll is free and pitch is blocked. The slot absorbs the small horizontal shift as the beam rolls.
  8. Beam → yokeThe yoke bolts on top of the beam, poles up, with non-magnetic fasteners. The lasers mount on the beam beside each pole, recessed 13.5 mm below the pole faces.
  9. CounterweightsOne cable from each end of the beam runs up over two ball-bearing pulleys to a 1.5 kg weight outside the frame. A hanging weight pulls with the same force at every gap, so the tare removes it, which a spring wouldn't allow. Putting one at each end keeps it from adding a roll moment.
  10. Gap → plateThe steel plate bolts under the frame top and can be removed for the zero tare. It must match the track's steel grade and thickness. G10 hard stops hanging from the plate set the minimum gap at 3 mm or more.

Mounting offset

Mount each laser so gap = reading − 13.5 mm:

  • A 16 mm reading means 2.5 mm, just below the hard stop.
  • A 26 mm reading means 12.5 mm, past the crossover, so the sweep never leaves the range.

That puts each laser's front face 13.5 mm below the pole-face plane. Set it with a 13.5 mm gauge block, then confirm with the shim-zero step in bring-up.

Target

A triangulation laser needs a diffuse, bright spot, not a dark one. Bare steel is shiny, which makes readings noisy. Put white matte tape or paint on the plate where each beam lands, since Baumer specifies the sensor on a white target. Black tape would lose signal.

Right-side up is easier on the hardware than inverted, because gravity subtracts from the pull. The motor works hardest when lowering the beam near 3 mm, against the full net pull, and it still has margin there. Closing the gap is helped by the load, and the self-locking screw keeps it from running away. If power drops, the screws hold. The hard stops cover the snap-in case anyway.

Sensing and control

What's measured

  • gap = (g_L + g_R) / 2, where g = reading − 13.5 mm for each laser, refined by shim-zero.
  • roll = (g_L − g_R) / b, where b is the laser spacing. At b ≈ 100 mm, a 10 µm difference is about 0.006°.
  • Cells L and R, each through its own HX711 at 10 SPS. F_z = (L + R) − tare, where the tare is taken with the plate removed and the counterweights attached. L − R gives the roll moment.
  • Pitch isn't measured. It's set once at assembly.

The two control loops are independent

  • Gap loop: move both actuators the same number of steps.
  • Roll loop: move them by opposite amounts.
  • The loop is quasi-static: no real-time control, just repeated move, settle, read, correct.
  • Approach every setpoint from the same side (from a larger gap) so any remaining nut play sits the same way each time.
for g_target in sweep (9.5 → 3.0 → 9.5 mm, 0.25 mm steps, ×3):
    repeat:
        move_both(steps_for(g_target − gap))          # gap loop
        move_diff(steps_for(roll · b / 2))             # roll loop, drive roll → 0
        wait(dwell)                                     # cell creep: 0.2 N / 5 min
        read lasers, cells (5 s @ 10 SPS), temperature
    until |gap − g_target| < 10 µm and |roll| < 0.1°
    log(t, g_L, g_R, gap, roll, cell_L, cell_R, F_z, temp)

Bring-up sequence

  1. Frame, plate, and screening. Bolt the plate under the top beam so it can come off. Check every part within 150 mm of the poles with a magnet.
  2. Beam on the actuators without the yoke. Home both actuators to the bottom. Check that the joints move freely and that the cells see only the beam's weight.
  3. Calibrate the cells in the working direction, using the pulleys. In the sweep the cells see an upward pull. Hanging masses from the beam would load them the other way. Instead, add known masses to the counterweight hangers, in 5–6 steps up to about 140 N per side, then remove them. That applies a known upward force through the real load path. Fit each cell's gain separately (they vary ±15%). Load and unload both ways, so the small pulley friction shows up as a known hysteresis band.
  4. Mount the yoke, attach the 1.5 kg counterweights, remove the plate, and tare. That reading is the zero. At 30 mm the magnet would still pull several newtons, so only a plate-off tare is a true zero.
  5. Set the laser offsets. Refit the plate. Set each laser 13.5 mm below its pole face with a gauge block. Then shim-zero: put identical non-magnetic shims between each pole and the plate, and raise the yoke until the cells start to change, which means the shims are clamped. That fixes each laser's offset to its own pole.
  6. Set pitch. Use feeler gauges front and back at one gap, then repeat at a small gap. If pitch changes with gap, the frame is flexing; stiffen it.
  7. Install the hard stops at 3.5 mm for the first runs. Lower them to 3 mm once the Ansys 2–4 mm results confirm the cell margin.
  8. Sweep. Run 9.5 → 3 → 9.5 mm three times, with roll held under 0.1° by the loop. Accept the data only if F is positive and decreases monotonically with gap, the sweeps repeat within a stated band, and zero drift stays below a stated limit.

Open items

  • Weigh the moving parts (yoke, beam, joints, cells, lasers). 6 kg is an estimate, and it sets both the crossover and the counterweight size. The rule: pick C so that F(9.7 mm) + C − W is at least 20 N.
  • Ansys results at 2–4 mm (pyaedt handoff, issue #1). These set the hard-stop height and the cell margin near contact.
  • The 2× reference disagreement. The new Ansys script and the §8 "per-yoke" curve differ by about 2×. Settle which definition you're testing against before you compare data.
  • Pulley friction. Measure it once, by loading and unloading through the pulleys at the calibration step. If it's more than about 0.5 N, use better bearings or bigger sheaves.
  • Laser cables and teach-in. Confirm the M8 cables are on hand, and that the analog output is scaled to the full 16–26 mm range (factory default, or set by teach-in).
  • Measure cell deflection under load once. The laser removes it from the gap reading, but it's worth knowing.
