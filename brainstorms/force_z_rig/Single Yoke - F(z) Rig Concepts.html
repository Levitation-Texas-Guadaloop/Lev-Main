Single-yoke F(z) rig: load cells, layouts, and magnet proximity

The goal is a calibrated, reproducible zero-current force-vs-gap curve for one small yoke, with the laser as the gap reference, to check against the Ansys script and the reluctance model. The Dec 2025 dataset failed on zero, gap, and tilt (Actuator Characterization §8). The plan below targets those three failures directly.

Short answers

Q1
Yes, with three conditions from the datasheet. The YZC-133 20 kg is an aluminum bar cell, and once calibrated its errors stay under 1% of the force at 5 mm. The three conditions: rated output is only ±15%, so every cell needs its own in-place calibration; safe overload is just 120% (235 N per cell), so hard stops are required; and the cell is specified for loading in one direction only. S-beams are better for a clean axial tension path at high load, which is why LEV-90 uses them at 1300 N. At this scale, a third YZC-133 for a 3-point mount helps more than switching to S-beams.
Q2
Four layouts are below. I recommend D, the two-actuator roll rig. The yoke rides on a beam, a stepper actuator at each end sets gap and roll, and two lasers (one per pole) close the loop. Joints lock pitch, which is safe on a 1" yoke. It controls exactly what two lasers can measure and runs scripted sweeps. B (an inverted 3-point hang) remains the better rehearsal for LEV-90.
Q3
The magnet won't bother the strain gauges at 1/2", but it will pull on any steel near them. A static field doesn't change a foil-gauge bridge in any meaningful way. The risk is ferromagnetic hardware on the frame side of the cell. A steel part there adds a gap-dependent force that a tare cannot remove. The threaded rod next to the left coil in the photo looks like steel. With coils on, heat and PWM pickup are added risks.

Q1 · Is this the right load cell?

Expected forces, from scripts/yoke_model.py (Ansys, zero current): 120 N @ 5 mm, 57 N @ 10 mm, 33 N @ 15 mm. Near contact (2–3 mm) is unsolved and probably 200–300 N. A 20 kg cell is 196 N full scale, so two or three cells sharing the load sit at 20–60% FS at the design gaps. That range is good for resolution.

Whatever cell you use, the Dec 2025 failures were in the system around it: a drifting zero, probably a second load path, hand-typed gaps, and uncontrolled tilt. Those get fixed in the mounting and the calibration protocol below, not by changing cells.

Q3 · Does a magnet 1/2" from the cell corrupt the reading?

A load cell reports every force between its live side and its dead side. The question is what else the magnet can add to that path.

One-minute test on the current rig: take the track off and tare. Then sweep a loose steel bar of about track size around the cells and brackets, not over the poles, and watch the reading. Anything above the noise means a magnetic load path on the dead side.

Q2 · Three rig layouts, with the laser as ground truth

  • aluminum / non-magnetic
  • steel (track-grade plate)
  • yoke
  • load cell
  • laser
  • G10 / shims / stops

Load direction differs between layouts. The YZC-133 is specified for force along its label arrow. In B, the magnet pulls the hanging plate down, so each cell loads like a kitchen scale, in the specified direction. In A and C, the magnet pulls the yoke up off its cells. The cells unload and, near contact, go into reverse (tension). The bridge still reads in reverse, but the datasheet doesn't cover it. Calibrate in the direction you'll use, or flip the cells.

All three follow one rule for the laser: mount it on the same body as the pole faces, or on the fixed base when the yoke is fixed. It then reads the true gap, including cell and frame deflection. At 5 mm, 0.1 mm of hidden deflection is about a 2% force error.

Concept A

Hanging-yoke Z-carriage (an upgrade of today's rig)

Good

  • Same geometry as the pod (yoke under the track) and reuses the current cells and brackets.
  • Gap sweeps continuously with one screw.

Weak

  • Two cells leave pitch to the carriage rails, so rail play becomes tilt error.
  • Roll is nulled only through rail alignment, with no fine adjustment.
  • The carriage holds the full pull, so the lead screw needs a positive lock.
Concept B

Inverted kinematic hang (LEV-90 at 1/10 scale)

Good

  • Three points are kinematic, so height, pitch, and roll set independently. Roll stays below 0.7° by turning one knob.
  • Three cells give Fz, both moments, and the center of force.
  • Cells hang far above the poles on rods, away from the magnet, and are loaded along the label arrow.
  • Three cells share up to 705 N at the safe-overload limit, compared with 470 N for two.
  • Uses the same procedure as LEV-90 (shim-zero, hard stops, flexures), so mistakes cost little here.

Weak

  • New build: frame, plate, and three adjusters.
  • Needs a third bar cell and a third HX711. Both are cheap.
  • The plate stands in for the track, so it must match the track's steel grade and thickness or it saturates and reads low.
Concept C

Spacer posts with shim stacks (fastest to first data)

Good

  • Stable by construction with no moving stage, so gaps repeat exactly.
  • Can be built in days from the current cells.
  • Its base, plate, laser bracket, and riser carry over to B. Swapping the posts for screws turns C into B.

Weak

  • Gaps are discrete, and each shim swap means lifting the plate against up to about 300 N. Jack screws help.
  • Roll is set only by shim matching.
  • Plate weight must stay off the cells, so the posts sit on the base, never on the yoke.
Concept D · recommended

Two-actuator roll rig, closed loop on two lasers

The yoke rides on an aluminum beam under a fixed steel plate. A stepper-driven actuator at each end of the beam sets gap and roll, and two lasers, one per pole, close the loop. Pitch is locked by the joints. With only two lasers, this rig controls exactly what it can measure.

Mechanics

  • The pin axes run into the page. A pin allows rotation only about its own axis, so roll is free and pitch is blocked.
  • One joint is a pin in a slot so the beam can slide a little horizontally. Without it the two pins over-constrain the beam as it rolls. The shift is about 0.02 mm at 1°, but it would put a side load on the cells.
  • Each cell sits in series with its actuator. All vertical load goes through the cells. Nothing passive carries force without being measured.
  • The magnet pulls the yoke up, so the cells are in tension. Mount them with the label arrow along that pull.
  • Use a stepper on a fine, non-backdrivable lead screw, for example a T8 with 2 mm lead, about 10 µm per full step. The magnet's pull preloads the screw in one direction, which takes up backlash. Approach every setpoint from the same side anyway. Cheap 12 V gear-motor actuators have 0.1–0.5 mm of backlash, which is a 4–10% force error at 5 mm.
  • Keep motors and steel lead screws ≥150 mm from the poles and drive the yoke through aluminum pushrods. Keep the hard stops and add software travel limits.

Sensing and control

  • Put one laser at each pole, on the beam (live side), aimed at matte tape. Shim-zero each laser to its own pole face.
  • gap = (g_L + g_R)/2 and roll = (g_L − g_R)/b. At b ≈ 100 mm, 10 µm of laser difference is about 0.006°, so holding roll under 0.7° is easy.
  • The two control loops are independent. Move both actuators together to change gap, or in opposite directions to change roll.
  • The loop is quasi-static, not real time: move, wait out creep, read lasers and cells, correct, log. Up/down sweeps run from a script, which removes hand-typed gaps.
  • The two cells give Fz = L + R and the roll moment. Pitch moment isn't measured, which is fine while pitch is locked.

Why locking pitch is safe on a 1" yoke

A pitch angle θ tilts the 25.4 mm deep pole face by about ±12.7·θ (θ in radians). Because force goes roughly as 1/g², a tilt that averages out symmetrically changes force only at second order, by about (a/g)², where a is the edge offset.

Set pitch once at assembly with feeler gauges. Then check it by moving one laser front to back, at a large gap and again at a small one. If pitch changes with gap, the structure is flexing under load. Stiffen the frame instead of adding a third actuator.

Calibration protocol (all layouts)

This is the part the Dec 2025 run skipped. The steps run in order.

  1. Check the mounting. One end of each cell bolted, the other end free. Nothing touches the white gauge cover. Leads are strain-relieved on the fixed side.
  2. In-situ dead-weight calibration, one cell at a time (gain spreads ±15%). Fully assembled, magnet in place, plate removed. Load known masses through the real load path, in the direction the magnet will pull, in 5–6 steps up to about 150 N per cell, staying under the 235 N safe overload. Then unload. Use the same dwell time at every step. Fit gain per cell and record nonlinearity and hysteresis.
  3. Load-path check. Move one mass across several positions. The sum of all cells must stay within about 1%. If it drifts, something else is carrying load, such as cables, a rubbing guide, or steel hardware.
  4. True zero at infinite gap. Remove the plate or track, or set it ≥40 mm away, and tare. Re-tare before and after every sweep and report the drift.
  5. Shim-zero the gap. Place non-magnetic shims on the pole faces and lower the plate until the cell reading starts to change. That sets the laser offset against the pole faces.
  6. Sweep. Go from 30 mm down to the hard stop and back up, three times, in 0.5 mm steps below 8 mm. At each point, wait a fixed dwell (creep is 0.2 N over 5 min), then log about 5 s of laser gap, all cells, roll, and temperature at 10 SPS. Bracket 3 mm only after the hard stops and overload margin are confirmed.
  7. Accept only if F is positive everywhere, decreases monotonically with gap, sweeps repeat within a stated band, and zero drift stays below a stated limit.

Flags before building

  • The two force references disagree by about 2×. The new Ansys script gives 85.8 N @ 7 mm. The "per-yoke" Ansys curve in Characterization §8 gives 43.8 N @ 7 mm. That doc's "half-baseline" wording suggests §8 counts one magnet leg pair rather than the whole yoke. Settle the definition before comparing any measurements.
  • Ansys points at 2–4 mm are missing (handoff open issue #1). Those points set hard-stop height and cell overload margin, so solve them first.
  • The threaded rod beside the left coil in yoke-rig.html looks like zinc-plated steel. Check it with a magnet.
  • Laser model and target. LEV-91 says to find the lasers first. Triangulation lasers read bare steel poorly, so put a matte patch of paint or tape on the plate where the beam lands.
  • The YZC-133 datasheet leaves out deflection. Measure it once under load so the stiffness margin is known rather than assumed. The magnet's negative stiffness is about 20 N/mm at 5 mm.
  • Inspect the current white shrouds for contact with the free end or the gauge cover. That would be a second load path, and it is the cheapest explanation for the broken Dec 2025 zero.
  • The YZC-133 and HX711 specs belong in LEV-88 (Load Cell Prereqs).
