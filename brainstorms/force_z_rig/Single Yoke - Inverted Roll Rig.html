Inverted roll rig: two steppers, two lasers, yoke hanging over the plate

The build detail for Concept D from the Single-Yoke F(z) Rig page, flipped upside down. Two lead-screw steppers hang the yoke beam over a fixed steel plate and set gap and roll. Two lasers, one per pole, close the loop. Pitch is locked by the joints. This page covers the geometry, why it's inverted, the actuator choice, control, and a parts list.

Axes

  • Roll is rotation about the into-the-page axis: left pole closer than right. controlled
  • Pitch is rotation about the horizontal axis: front edge versus back edge. locked by joints
  • Yaw is rotation about the vertical axis. Gap doesn't depend on it. ignored
  • The view in every drawing is the side view from the concept pages: the yoke's long direction runs left to right.

Why inverted

The yoke and beam weigh about 6 kg (59 N). In the upright version (yoke under the plate), the magnet pulls up and gravity pulls down, so they work against each other. Flipping the rig puts both forces in the same direction.

Load path, top to bottom (each side)

  1. Frame top beamAluminum (80/20 or plate). It carries everything, so make it stiff. Flex shows up as pitch.
  2. StepperA 48 mm NEMA 17 bolted under the top beam, at least 150 mm above the poles. Its internal bearing takes the axial load.
  3. Lead screw + nutTr8×2, so 10 µm per full step. The thread is self-locking, so the load can't back-drive it. The anti-backlash nut is a bonus here, since gravity already preloads the nut.
  4. MGN12 carriageHolds the nut so it can't turn and keeps it moving straight. The rail takes side loads and pitch moment so the cells don't.
  5. Aluminum pushrodNon-magnetic link from the carriage down toward the magnet.
  6. Load cell (YZC-133)Fixed end bolted to the pushrod bracket, free end to the joint. Mount it with the label arrow pointing down, so it sees load in its specified direction. It is always in tension and never crosses zero.
  7. JointLeft: pin. Right: pin in a slot. Both pin axes run into the page, so roll is free and pitch is blocked. The slot absorbs the small horizontal shift as the beam rolls.
  8. Beam → yokeThe yoke bolts under the beam with poles down, using non-magnetic fasteners. The lasers mount on the beam beside each pole.
  9. Gap → plateThe steel plate sits on aluminum standoffs on the base and can be removed for the zero tare. It must match the track's steel grade and thickness. G10 hard stops on the plate set the minimum gap at 3 mm or more.

Actuator

Pick

StepperOnline 17LS19-0406E-300Y, about $68.49 plus shipping and tariff.

  • External (rotating) Tr8×2 screw, 2 mm lead, 300 mm long. Cut it down or leave it.
  • 48 mm NEMA 17, 32 N·cm holding torque, 0.4 A/phase, 12 V coil.
  • Anti-backlash nut included.

Alternatives are parked for later: a VXB ball-screw stage, which costs more and back-drives, and an Actuonix P16, which has 0.3 mm backlash. A mid-range option is the Anaheim 17AW302CX06-LW4-EA at $182, but its lead isn't confirmed.

Near 3 mm, lifting the beam (opening the gap) works against the full load, so that is where the motor is weakest. Lowering is helped by the load, and self-locking stops it running away. To get margin near contact, raise driver current within the motor rating, keep moves slow, or set the hard stops at 3.5 mm. Above 4 mm there's plenty of margin.

Sensing and control

What's measured

  • Lasers L and R on the beam, pointing down at matte tape on the plate. Each is shim-zeroed to its own pole face.
  • gap = (g_L + g_R) / 2
  • roll = (g_L − g_R) / b, where b is the laser spacing. At b ≈ 100 mm, a 10 µm difference is about 0.006°.
  • Cells L and R, each through its own HX711 at 10 SPS. F_z = L + R − W, with W tared with the plate removed. The difference L − R gives the roll moment.
  • Pitch isn't measured. It's set once at assembly.

The two control loops are independent

  • Gap loop: move both actuators the same number of steps.
  • Roll loop: move them by opposite amounts.
  • The loop is quasi-static: there's no real-time control, just iterations of move, settle, read, correct.
  • Always approach a setpoint from the same direction (for example from a larger gap) so any remaining nut play sits the same way every time.
for g_target in sweep (30 → 3.5 → 30 mm, ×3):
    repeat:
        move_both(steps_for(g_target − gap))          # gap loop
        move_diff(steps_for(roll · b / 2))             # roll loop, drive roll → 0
        wait(dwell)                                     # creep: 0.2 N / 5 min
        read lasers, cells (5 s @ 10 SPS), temperature
    until |gap − g_target| < 10 µm and |roll| < 0.1°
    log(t, g_L, g_R, gap, roll, cell_L, cell_R, F_z, temp)

Bring-up sequence

  1. Frame and plate. Fix the plate to the base on standoffs and level it. Check every part within 150 mm of the poles with a magnet.
  2. Hang the beam without the yoke. Home both actuators to the top. Check that the joints move freely and that the cells read only the beam's weight.
  3. Calibrate the cells in place. Hang known masses from the beam through the real load path, up to about 150 N per side. Fit each cell's gain separately (±15% spread) and check the sum stays constant as the mass moves.
  4. Tare with the plate removed. This is the true zero, W alone. At 30 mm the magnet still pulls several newtons (the old Ansys curve shows about 4 N per half-yoke at 29–31 mm), so the largest gap isn't a zero. Make the plate slide out on the base, or lift it off, for this step.
  5. Shim-zero the lasers. Put identical non-magnetic shims on the plate under each pole. Lower the yoke until the cells start to unload, which means the yoke is resting on the shims. That sets each laser's offset to its own pole.
  6. Set pitch. Use feeler gauges front and back at one gap, then repeat at a large and a small gap. If pitch changes with gap, the frame is flexing; stiffen it.
  7. Install the hard stops at 3.5 mm for the first runs.
  8. Sweep. Run 30 → 3.5 → 30 mm three times, with roll held under 0.1° by the loop. Accept the data only if F is positive and decreases monotonically with gap, the sweeps repeat within a stated band, and zero drift stays below a stated limit.

Open items

  • Laser model and range. The laser's measuring span limits the sweep. For example, a Keyence IL-030 spans about 25 mm, so mount height decides whether 3.5–30 mm fits. Check whether the lasers found for LEV-91 are the Baumer OM20 in the datasheets folder.
  • Ansys results at 2–4 mm (pyaedt handoff, issue #1). These set the hard-stop height and the thrust margin near contact.
  • Weigh the yoke and beam. 6 kg is an estimate, and W sets the per-side load at every gap.
  • The 2× reference disagreement. The new Ansys script and the §8 "per-yoke" curve differ by about 2×. Settle which definition you're testing against before you compare data.
  • Measure cell deflection under load once. The laser removes it from the gap reading, but it's worth knowing.
